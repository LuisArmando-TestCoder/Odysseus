# Existential Risk Investigations

A structured, iterative investigation of threats to human (civilizational and biological) survival. Each threat is worked **one at a time, to completion**, through a fixed loop:

**definition (first principles) → systems analysis → homeostatic/homeodynamic interventions (short/medium/long) → adversarial critique & refinement → cross-domain benchmarking → synthesis.**

## The loop (the unit of work)

1. **Definition (first principles).** Reduce the threat to its necessary and sufficient causal components. State explicitly what would *have* to be true for extinction (distinct from mere catastrophe).
2. **Systems analysis.** Explicit stock-and-flow / causal-loop account: reinforcing (R) and balancing (B) feedbacks, delays, nonlinearities, plus Meadows leverage points ranked by power.
3. **Interventions, three horizons, two regimes.**
   - Horizons: **Short (0–5y), Medium (5–20y), Long (20–50y+).**
   - **Homeostatic** = negative-feedback measures that detect perturbation and restore a *fixed* safe baseline (detect-damp-repair). The system returns to where it was.
   - **Homeodynamic** = anticipatory/adaptive measures that re-tune the system's reference point and evolve with a *changing* environment. The defense co-evolves with the threat.
4. **Adversarial critique.** Steelman the strongest objection to each intervention (technical, incentive, governance, dual-use), then refine.
5. **Benchmarking.** Map each intervention to an analogous domain that has solved (or failed to solve) a structurally similar problem; extract the transferable mechanism.
6. **Synthesis.** The refined portfolio, layered as defense-in-depth, with the homeodynamic core identified.

## Protocol

Proceed one threat at a time. Do not look at the whole list; the loop above is the unit of work.

## Operational definitions

- **Homeostatic (balance-restoring):** negative feedback. Sensors detect deviation; effectors counteract; the desired state ("no outbreak") is fixed. *Strengths:* fast, reliable, legible. *Weaknesses:* assumes the perturbation is transient and the environment static; can be overwhelmed if loop capacity is exceeded; does nothing to shrink the *source* of perturbation.
- **Homeodynamic (adaptive):** the setpoint is permitted to move; the system is engineered to continuously re-infer its goal and re-configure. *Strengths:* robust to a changing threat landscape; turns change into advantage. *Weaknesses:* harder to build and verify; the adaptation machinery is itself a dual-use surface.
