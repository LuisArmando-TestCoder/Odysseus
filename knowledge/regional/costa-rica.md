## Who holds what (Oct 2026)
- President: **Laura Fernández** ("gobierno de la continuidad", successor of the Chaves administration).
- **Rodrigo Chaves** (former president, 2022–2026) is Minister of the Presidency AND heads Hacienda — one person shapes both the budget and the Executive's political message.

## Live systemic threads (use for inference; each is a pattern, not proof)
- **Executive vs Judiciary budget war.** 2027 budget bill gives the Judiciary ₡530.973,2 M (−₡10.552,5 M, −1,9%) with ₡29.843 M conditioned on Hacienda; execution norms 9–12 target only the Judiciary (trust-fund oversight, transfer limits, salary conditions, freeze on filling 2.463 "vacant" posts — 810 in the OIJ — that are actually held by interim staff). The Court accepted a ₡13.242 M cut for 2026 but rejected the extra 2027 cut. Presidencia itself gets ~+₡2.000 M, comparable to what the OIJ asks for new offices (Cabo Velas, La Cruz). Open: Hacendarios vote on norms 9–12; Sala IV on judicial independence.
- **The judicial trust fund (fideicomiso).** Officials cited $600 → $700 → $771 M "cash"; financial statements show **$90,8 M** liquid at 31 Jul 2026, the rest tied to 17 projects; the OIJ asked for staff and rent, not buildings. Expect "exists ≠ available" and apples-and-oranges sums (deposit interest + guarantees + emergency fund).
- **Inaugurated but not staffed.** CCSS audits (AD-AOPER-0003-2025; AS-ASALUD-0057-2026) link 1,4 M people on waiting lists to idle operating rooms/beds for lack of staff; Puntarenas hospital reportedly usable at 5/14 operating rooms and 7/24 ICU beds (Undeca via La Nación); Hospital México tower may open short-staffed. CCSS created 2.316 nursing posts 2023–2026 but has no technical study of how many are missing. Puntarenas and Turrialba hospitals were contracted/started 2019–2022 (Alvarado); only Carrillo and Aguas Zarcas were entirely within the Chaves period. Pattern: planning for the ribbon-cutting, not for operation. Open: how many of the announced ₡1,2 billones portfolio already have approved posts.
- **Communication format.** Most checked claims originate in the weekly press conference or national broadcasts (cadena nacional), a format with little time for follow-up questions; Casa Presidencial, Hacienda and MEP typically do not answer verification requests. Cost of verification shifts to the public and small newsrooms.
- **Pressure on checks and balances.** Estado de la Justicia (5th report) counted 16 Executive attacks on the Judiciary 2022–2024 and links the strategy to democratic backsliding — while also criticising the Court's "cortecentric" model and asking for better accountability. Legitimate criticism and false claims get mixed; separate them.
- **Education.** PISA 2025: Costa Rica 58, 45 and 76 points below the OECD average; OECD uses ~20 points ≈ one school year (not 13); CR's top 25% ranked 36 of 38. Real improvement in science exists — acknowledge it before measuring exaggeration.
- **Universities.** UCR scholarship adjustments are evaluated every year for the 2nd semester (starts ~10 Aug); ₡350.000 is a monthly total cap, adjustments are percentage-based — beware "cap presented as increase" and "annual adjustment presented as reaction to a protest".
- **Media.** Some digital outlets publish unsourced claims (e.g., Impact Channel on Cine Magaly/'Naza' — director admitted having no proof) and respond to checks by attacking the checker.
